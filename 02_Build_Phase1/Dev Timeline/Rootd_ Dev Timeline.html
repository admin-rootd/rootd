Rootd: Dev Timeline

Status: v0.2 draft, 8 Oct 2026 (project started 1 Oct 2026, now in week 2). Owner: Ace. Tracker: GitHub Projects.

1. Assumptions

  • Capacity: 1-2 hours a day, about 5-10 hours a week. AI does most analysis, breakdown and coding. Ace decides, reviews and tests.
  • Order: Standalone (PR / Citizenship) first, local development only. Full Pathway (NLV / DNV) follows. Mobile comes last.
  • Estimates are planning figures without buffer. Add about 20% (see section 7). Legal review time depends on the attorney.
  • Start date: 1 Oct 2026. The dates below are targets without buffer. December holidays will likely slow things down, so treat late December and early January as soft.

7. Risks and buffer

  • Buffer: add about 20% to each phase. Standalone about 15-16 weeks, Full Pathway about 15-16 weeks.
  • Legal review turnaround is outside your control. Book early and send work in small batches.
  • Review is the bottleneck: AI can build faster than 1-2 hours a day can review. Keep tasks small so each review fits in one session.
  • Real-data testing needs consent and careful handling. Never commit real data.
  • Unclear rules: if a rule is ambiguous, stop and ask the attorney instead of guessing.

8. Open decisions

  1. Document uploads: later, tied to retention and encryption choices.
  2. Standalone pricing model.
  3. AI provider behind the wrapper.
  4. Final app name, then trademark and domain check.
  5. Entity and jurisdiction.
  6. How to sell on mobile (web-only purchase or in-app purchase).

9. GitHub Projects setup

Fields: Status (Not started, In progress, Blocked, Done), Milestone (M0-M15), Owner (AI, Ace, Both, Lawyer), Track (standalone, full-pathway, later), Target week.

Views: Table grouped by milestone (planning), Board by Status (daily use), Roadmap by Target week (the Gantt equivalent).

Labels: standalone, full-pathway, later, owner:ai, owner:ace, owner:both, owner:lawyer.

Seeding: seed_github_project.sh creates the milestones and one issue per task above, and adds them to your project. It runs as a dry run by default and prints the commands. It has not been tested against a real repository, so check the dry-run output first.

Weekly rhythm: one 30-minute review on a fixed day. Update statuses, move blocked items, pick next week's tasks, and check the critical path.
